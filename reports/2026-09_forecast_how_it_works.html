S2 · Will the sewers overflow?

Inputs, weights, output. The 5 numbers are the inputs: what goes in each day, computed from the rain and the wind. The weights are what the fit learned, 9 per basin, fixed since training: one for each input and one for each bend. Which inputs and bends it uses was picked by nested selection from a menu of them, the same in every basin; the fit learns how much each matters. Every station in a basin shares that basin's S2; the beaches differ in S3 and S4. The output is one number per basin per day: the chance the sewers overflow. One model per combined-sewer basin, trained on the days SFPUC filed an overflow (CIWQS from 2016, and SFPUC's own monthly reports from 2011 where CIWQS is silent). Each is a weights model: its 9 terms (5 inputs and 4 bends at a few rain amounts, so the response can steepen), standardised, then an L2 logistic fit. Every basin reads the gauge that predicted it best on the holdout. The two charts are the served models answering "how likely is an overflow today if this much falls?", once after a dry month and once after a wet start to the week.

The math behind the weights

The model. For a basin and a day, take 5 inputs (today's rain total, last 2 days, last 3 days, the south wind while it rains (m/s; from the north is negative), wettest 3 hours) and 4 bends on them: for an input f and a knot k, hf,k = max(0, xf − k), which is zero until the input passes the knot and then rises with it. Which inputs and bends it uses was picked by nested selection from a menu of them (src/models/train_terms.py), the same in every basin; the fit learns only the weights. That is how "the first quarter inch barely matters, the next inch matters a lot" becomes a straight-line model. Together these are the 9 terms t1 … t9.

Standardise. Each term is centred and scaled by its training mean and standard deviation: zj = (tj − μj) / σj. So every weight below is "per one standard deviation of that term", and weights are comparable across terms.

Combine. The log-odds of an overflow is a weighted sum:  log(p / (1 − p)) = β0 + Σj βj zj,  and the probability is  p = 1 / (1 + e−(β0 + Σ βj zj)). A weight of +0.5 multiplies the odds by e0.5 ≈ 1.65 for each standard deviation of its term. The "share" in the table above is |βj| / Σ|β|.

Fit. The weights minimise the log-loss over the training days plus a penalty (1 / 2C) Σ βj², the L2 ridge: a smaller C shrinks all weights toward zero and spreads credit across correlated terms (today's total and the last two days move together, so they share weight). C is chosen per basin by leave-one-season-out cross-validation on the seasons before the holdout. Serving adds a calibration offset that fades to zero by half an inch of three-day rain; for this family the offset is 0, so p is used as fitted.

Even three to five terms hold within a few points. The 19 inputs are cheap to compute, so the cost of the extra terms is not speed; it is that the model is harder to explain and that correlated terms share credit in ways that shift between fits. That test has been run: the live model since 2026-10-07 is a short list, its terms picked by nested selection inside each season's fold (src/models/train_terms.py).

S3 · Which beaches?

An overflow reaches the beaches its outfalls post, and not every outfall spills when its basin does. Outfall split (stored as stage 2 v2_d10) gives each beach group its basin's chance times the share of the basin's overflow days, by size, on which the group's own outfalls spilled: Ocean Beach and Aquatic Park do not see every overflow in their basin. Where a group's outfalls are the whole basin, the share is 1. The day's size comes from S2's volume heads; between small and large the share is blended.

S5 · Live corrections: Basin flags

Once the forecast is running, what SFPUC's beach map and the city's lab results say can override the model, and only then: with nothing observed the output is the model alone, to the digit. Every change is recorded and shown on the page as a badge.

An overflow flag appears

That basin's day becomes 100% and the beach tail follows. If the day before had rain of 0.25" or a model chance of 25%, it is anchored too, because the feed's flag usually lands a day late.

The flag stays up

Up for 2 days means a large overflow: the day moves to the large-event curve, and the beaches hold there while the flag is up (at most 7 days).

No flag when one was expected

A bay-basin day the model put at 15% or more that the feed never flagged is downgraded, more with each quiet day. Never on the Westside: the feed's record there is unmeasured. Only while the watcher is live.

A sample comes back over standard

Within a week of an overflow it floors the beach at how often the next bottle is also bad, but only where that is 50% or more: today the East, at 80%. In dry weather it changes nothing.

A sample comes back clean

Within a week of an overflow it caps the lingering part of the risk at how often a clean bottle is followed by a bad one. Never zero.

How it was trained

The labels

SFPUC's filed overflow reports, one row per outfall per event: 1,104 events on 112 days, 2016-10-16 → 2026-04-22, pulled from CIWQS each quarter. Bay-side basins also learn from the 2016-17 feed archive's flags; the Westside does not, because those flags lag the rain.

The choice

A leaderboard fit 40 combinations of model family, rain gauge and regularisation per basin, judged by leave-one-season-out cross-validation before the holdout and then by holdout ranking. The weights model won every basin on the holdout and became S2 (9 terms with the south wind and the 3-hour peak); Outfall split (S3) and Linger table 2, more samples (S4) were added; the set, ICON · 9 terms with the south wind and the 3-hour peak · Outfall split · Linger table 2, more samples · Basin flags, was promoted on 2026-10-07.

What is kept

The retired set, ICON · 38-weight · Outfall split · Linger table 2, more samples · Basin flags (stored as icon-w38-osplit-lt2more-bflags), and 24 other candidate sets stay on disk with their own scorecards, so the Model check can grade them on the same days; each has its own S2 page, listed with every report. Nothing is retrained on the fly: the pickles change only at a promotion.
