  2. Rain in, liveTwo NOAA gauges for every past day, the SFO gauge for today's hours, the ICON model for the hours ahead: one hourly series.
  3. 19 numbers a dayTotals, lags, peaks, dryness.
  4. Stage 1 · 4 basinsThe chance the sewers overflow today.
  5. Stage 2 · 6 beach groupsHow long, and which beaches: a percentage from the last eight days.
  6. Live corrections v2The beach map's flags and postings and this week's samples override the model where they have something to say.
  7. OutFour zones, six days, every 30 minutes; the alarm line at 25%; every forecast kept.
  • Do the gauges shape the live numbers? Yes, first of all. Every complete past day is re-based onto the two NOAA gauges before anything runs; the SFO airport gauge fills today's hours so far and the peak-hour numbers of recent days; the weather model only fills the hours that have not happened yet.
  • Does a new weather model mean retraining? No. The model reads inches. Swapping ICON for another model changes an input, never the fitted weights.
  • What if nothing is observed? Then the live corrections are the identity and the page shows the model alone, to the digit.
  • Where do the city's bacteria samples come in? Twice. Past samples taught stage 2 how long a beach stays dirty after an overflow (the dashed arrow). New samples, as the beach map and DataSF publish them, correct the live numbers (the solid one).

The math behind the weights

The model. For a basin and a day, take the 19 inputs x1 … x19 (inches, days, or a yes/no). Add 19 bends: for an input f and a knot k, hf,k = max(0, xf − k), which is zero until the input passes the knot and then rises with it. That is how "the first quarter inch barely matters, the next inch matters a lot" becomes a straight-line model. Together these are the 38 terms t1 … t38.

Standardise. Each term is centred and scaled by its training mean and standard deviation: zj = (tj − μj) / σj. So every weight below is "per one standard deviation of that term", and weights are comparable across terms.

Combine. The log-odds of an overflow is a weighted sum:  log(p / (1 − p)) = β0 + Σj βj zj,  and the probability is  p = 1 / (1 + e−(β0 + Σ βj zj)). A weight of +0.5 multiplies the odds by e0.5 ≈ 1.65 for each standard deviation of its term. The "share" in the table above is |βj| / Σ|β|.

Fit. The weights minimise the log-loss over the training days plus a penalty (1 / 2C) Σ βj², the L2 ridge: a smaller C shrinks all weights toward zero and spreads credit across correlated terms (today's total and the last two days move together, so they share weight). C is chosen per basin by leave-one-season-out cross-validation on the seasons before the holdout. Serving adds a calibration offset that fades to zero by half an inch of three-day rain; for this family the offset is 0, so p is used as fitted.

Could it be simpler?

Chase, 2026-09-30: "simpler is king". The same design refit with fewer terms, on the same days and labels (src/models/sparse_logit_eval.py, 2026-09-30 03:53). PR-AUC ranks overflow days above quiet ones (1.0 perfect); Brier is the squared error of the probability (lower is better). The holdout exam fits on the seasons before Jul 2023 and scores Jul 2023 → Oct 2025; the since-training exam fits through Oct 2025 and scores the days after. The "top K" rows rank terms by the served model, which itself saw the holdout, so read them as optimistic; the L1 and hand-picked rows carry no such advantage. Shaded: the served design.

Even three to five terms hold within a few points. The 19 inputs are cheap to compute, so the cost of the extra terms is not speed; it is that the model is harder to explain and that correlated terms share credit in ways that shift between fits. A candidate set with about five terms per basin, chosen by cross-validation on the seasons before the holdout, would be the honest test; it would go through the Model check and the analysis report like every candidate before it, and serving already handles the family.

Stage 2 · which beaches, and for how long?

An overflow fouls a beach for days. This stage is built from the city's bacteria samples: for each beach group, the lab results after past overflows give the chance the water is still over standard k days later, large and small overflows apart, with the dry-weather background removed. The outfall split (stage 2 v2) first scales the basin's chance by the share of its overflows that reach the group at all: Ocean Beach and Aquatic Park do not see every overflow in their basin. The percentage on the page is the combined risk from the last eight days.

Live corrections · live_v2

Once the forecast is running, what SFPUC's beach map and the city's lab results say can override the model, and only then: with nothing observed the output is the model alone, to the digit. Every change is recorded and shown on the page as a badge.

An overflow flag appears

That basin's day becomes 100% and the beach tail follows. If the day before had rain of 0.25" or a model chance of 25%, it is anchored too, because the feed's flag usually lands a day late.

The flag stays up

Up for 2 days means a large overflow: the day moves to the large-event curve, and the beaches hold there while the flag is up (at most 7 days).

No flag when one was expected

A bay-basin day the model put at 15% or more that the feed never flagged is downgraded, more with each quiet day. Never on the Westside: the feed's record there is unmeasured. Only while the watcher is live.

A sample comes back over standard

Within a week of an overflow it floors the beach at how often the next bottle is also bad, but only where that is 50% or more: today the East, at 80%. In dry weather it changes nothing.

A sample comes back clean

Within a week of an overflow it caps the lingering part of the risk at how often a clean bottle is followed by a bad one. Never zero.

A scheduler recomputes the forecast at :05 and :35, stores it, and the page always serves the stored copy. Each day's first and last forecast go to the history tables with the model's name, so a model swap never breaks the record. The Model check grades every stored day.

How it was trained

The labels

SFPUC's filed overflow reports, one row per outfall per event: 1,104 events on 112 days, 2016-10-16 → 2026-04-22, pulled from CIWQS each quarter. Bay-side basins also learn from the 2016-17 feed archive's flags; the Westside does not, because those flags lag the rain.

The choice

A leaderboard fit 40 combinations of model family, rain gauge and regularisation per basin, judged by leave-one-season-out cross-validation before the holdout and then by holdout ranking. The weights model won every basin on the holdout; the outfall split was added as stage 2 v2; the set was promoted on 2026-09-28 on the analysis report's cost ranking.

What is kept

The retired set (gb_v1, gradient-boosted trees) and two other candidates stay on disk with their own scorecards, so the Model check can grade them on the same days. Nothing is retrained on the fly: the pickles change only at a promotion.
