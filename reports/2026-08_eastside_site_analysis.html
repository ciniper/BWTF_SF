East-Side Shoreline Water Quality

Analysis of SF's public lab data (DataSF, SFPUC shoreline monitoring) · Jul 2020 – Aug 2026 · 21,004 lab results · "Bad" = Enterococcus ≥ 104 MPN/100 mL (the state single-sample standard — the same one BWTF grading uses) · Live interactive version: the dashboard's Site Report Card page (/analysis)
Bottom line: Week by week, Sunnydale Cove and Islais Creek are the east side's worst sites (roughly 1 in 5 routine weekly samples bad over the last two years, 1 in 3 during storm season), and Sunnydale has been worsening every year. Mission Creek's contamination is episodic — moderate week-to-week, but its bad stretches persist for days. India Basin — sitting between Islais Creek and Sunnydale Cove — is not sampled by the city at all. It's bracketed by the two worst east-side sites and is a genuine data gap that volunteer testing would uniquely fill.

Weekly = the first sample of each site-week (the city's routine Monday cadence), which is apples-to-apples with BWTF's own weekly volunteer sampling. *"Incl. resamples" adds the city's follow-up samples after exceedances; those cluster around contamination events, so they measure how often tests failed rather than how often weeks were bad. For scale, the west-side beaches BWTF currently covers run roughly 2–12% week-by-week (China Beach lowest; Baker Beach at Lobos Creek, ~12%, highest).

The Mission Creek nuance: including resamples, Mission Creek looks co-worst (32.7%); week-by-week it's mid-pack (12.5%). The gap is the signature of episodic contamination — when Mission goes bad, it stays bad for days and racks up failing follow-up tests before clearing. Its week-to-week base rate is moderate but rising (17.3% in 2025). Both views are true; they answer different questions.

Rain-driven discharge (combined-sewer + stormwater) dominates: in storm season Sunnydale and Islais fail roughly 1 in 3 weeks. Note the exception — Mission Creek is nearly as bad in the dry season as the wet, pointing to a non-storm source as well. That also speaks to the lead's storm-season staffing concern: the east side is worst exactly when volunteer capacity is tightest.

Sunnydale Cove is worsening steadily — from 4% of weeks (2020) to 27% (2025), across both wet and dry years. Islais stepped up in the 2023 storm year and has stayed elevated; Mission Creek is lower but climbing. All of these sites have recorded readings maxing out the lab assay (>24,196 MPN — 230× the standard).

The India Basin answer

The city's monitoring network has no station at India Basin — it is the largest unsampled gap on the east shoreline, sitting directly between Islais Creek and Sunnydale Cove, the two worst east-side sites week-by-week. The city's data cannot answer "how bad is India Basin" — but both neighbors say the stretch it sits on fails ~1 in 5 routine weekly samples, concentrated in storm season. That's a strong, evidence-based case that volunteer testing there would produce information nobody currently has.

Methodology: (1) Enterococcus vs the CA single-sample maximum (104 MPN/100mL); fecal coliform tells the same story. (2) "Weekly" keeps the first sample of each site-week — the routine schedule — and excludes post-exceedance resamples, which otherwise roughly double event-heavy sites' rates. (3) A few Ocean Beach stations (Vicente, Pacheco, Fort Funston) show higher raw rates but are sampled only sporadically/reactively (~50 samples vs ~400+ routine), so they're excluded from ranking claims. (4) The city's published dataset begins 2020-07-27; nothing earlier exists in it.
