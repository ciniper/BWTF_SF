SF Sewage Forecast — Model Performance

v2.1 · trained on SFPUC-reported discharge events · report generated 2026-08-21

The forecaster predicts, per drainage basin, the probability that San Francisco's combined sewer system discharges to the shoreline, and composes that with measured post-discharge beach contamination persistence to show sewage risk at the city's beaches. As of August 2026 it is trained on SFPUC's own per-event discharge records (1,007 events, Oct 2016 – Oct 2025, extracted from monthly self-monitoring reports filed with the Regional Water Board) rather than the bacteria-sample proxy used previously.

How long beaches stay contaminated after a discharge

Share of beach water samples still exceeding standards, by days since a larger-than-median discharge in the beach's basin (beach samples joined to the event record; isotonic-smoothed). This is the report's most consequential finding: Ocean Beach disperses fastest — Pacific surf clears it within a few days — while Baker/China and the Southeast bayside hold contamination most of a week. The dashboard uses these curves (split further by discharge size) to keep showing risk after the rain stops.

Dry-weather baselines (no discharge within 7 days) by group: Ocean Beach 19% · Baker-China 15% · Crissy Field 12% · Aquatic Park 5% · Southeast 14%. The displayed risk counts only the discharge-attributable excess over these baselines, so dry days read ~0%. Sparse groups (Crissy, Aquatic Park) have few post-event samples; missing buckets hold the prior value.

Why the retrain was necessary

The previous label — "3+ bacteria stations elevated across 2+ basins" from weekly beach samples — was measured against the real event record on the 637 overlapping sample days: 79% precision (1 in 5 positives had no discharge behind them — bird and runoff bacteria mislabeled as sewage) and 50% recall (half of the sample days that followed a real discharge were labeled clean). The old model was trained to alarm on noise and stay quiet on real events; every downstream number improved once the labels became SFPUC's own reports.

How the live number is computed

For each day the dashboard shows 1 − ∏ (1 − P(discharge k days ago) × persistence(k, discharge size)) over the prior week — discharge probability from rain (observed for past days, forecast ahead), persistence from the measured curves above, expected discharge size from a volume regression. Where the alert system's watcher has observed a CSO flag from SFPUC, that day's probability is replaced with certainty. Simulated test events are excluded at the query level.

Known limitations

  • Westside per-event records don't exist publicly before 2018 (records request drafted); Bayside coverage starts Oct 2016. Nov 2025+ filings aren't public on CIWQS yet.
  • Training intensity features use ERA5 reanalysis hourly rain; the live forecast uses weather-model hourly rain — same API family, but sharpness can differ in live storms.
  • Per-basin event counts are modest (32–78); season-to-season variance in the CV reflects that. Persistence curves for Crissy Field and Aquatic Park rest on few post-event samples.
  • Discharge prediction is citywide-rain-driven; localized cloudbursts over one basin are averaged by the two-gauge daily mean (hourly intensity features mitigate this).

Next steps

  • Tide, swell, and solar/UV as persistence-model features (flushing and sunlight set decay speed).
  • Per-indicator concentration targets (enterococcus / E. coli / total coliform) instead of binary exceedance.
  • Surface the per-beach-group risk breakout in the dashboard UI (already in the API payload).
  • Quarterly re-scrape of CIWQS as new monthly reports become public; records request for pre-2018 Westside events.
