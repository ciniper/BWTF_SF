Each row is a day. The chips are the forecast for that day as it developed, from five days out (left) to the morning of (right); a percentage is the chance the zone's beaches were affected by a sewer overflow, coloured by the page's four risk levels. A blue ring means a live rule fired that morning: a CSO flag, a lab result or a quiet feed moved the number. Hover a chip for the rain it was fed. To the right: whether SFPUC's map showed a posted or CSO flag at a station of the zone that day, how many stations the city sampled and how many were over standard, and the gauges' measured rain. The tally grades every day up to yesterday at the Medium edge ({{ (edges.medium * 100) | round | int }}% and up counts as an alert), per lead. These rulers are fast and imperfect — the map misses onsets and the city samples weekly — so this tab is a check, not the grade: the protocol grades the live season against the quarterly CIWQS ledger (grade_prospective.py).

The season so far, by lead

Days up to yesterday whose ruler is known. Feed: a posted or CSO flag at a station of the zone on SFPUC's map. Samples: a city sample over the single-sample standard that day. Surfrider: a volunteer sample over the same standard that day. Either: any of them. Caught = alerted and bad; missed = quiet and bad; false alarm = alerted and fine. Brier is the mean squared gap between the percentage and what happened (0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip).
