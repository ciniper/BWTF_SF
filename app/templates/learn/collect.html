Taking a sample

The short version. What happens once the jars reach the lab is in the Lab SOP.

  1. Write the site, date and time on the sample jar.
  2. Wade out until the water is about 2 feet deep, around your knees.
  3. Face the waves. If it's wavy, sample as a wave comes in.
  4. Uncap the jar without touching its rim or the inside of the lid.
  5. Push it mouth-down to about 6 inches under the surface, then tip it up to fill, away from your body.
  6. Cap it, keep it cold in the cooler, and note the conditions for the sheet.

Conditions for the sheet

The sheet's weather at the time you sampled, from public sources, in the sheet's own choices. Tap Edit on any of them to put in your own reading: your eyes and thermometer win.

Tide from NOAA's predictions for the beach, its level in feet above the average lowest low (MLLW); air, sky and wind from Open-Meteo at the site's pin; waves and water temperature from Open-Meteo's ocean model; rain from Open-Meteo's model hours, summed over the 72 hours before the time you picked. Times are Pacific; the sheet covers the last 90 days and the next week. Observations stay on this device, saved with the site and date, until you save the image.
