Where the rain goes

San Francisco's sewers are combined: what goes down the drain at home and the rain off the street share the same pipes. Move the slider, or play a storm.

None
NoneLightHeavyStorm

Swipe the diagram sideways to see the shore.

Dry
  1. 1Homes and businesses: everything down the drain, and the rain off the roofs.
  2. 2Storm drains: rain off the street.
  3. 3One combined sewer carries both, downhill to the shore. Over 90% of the city is served this way.
  4. 4Transport/storage structures ring the shoreline and hold what the plant cannot take yet.
  5. 5Pumped from the storage's floor to a treatment plant.
  6. 6When storage is full, the settled water passes under a baffle that holds back floating trash, over the outlet's weir and out an outfall at the shore: a combined sewer discharge.
  7. 7Treated water leaves through the plant's deep-water outfall: about four miles offshore for the Oceanside plant, into the bay for the Southeast plant.

Meet the indicators

Labs do not look for every germ in sewage. They count a few easy-to-grow gut bacteria that travel with it: when these are high, sewage or runoff is likely in the water.
