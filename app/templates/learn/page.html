Where the rain goes

San Francisco's sewers are combined: what goes down the drain at home and the rain off the street share the same pipes. Move the slider, or play a storm.

  1. 1Homes and businesses: everything down the drain, and the rain off the roofs.
  2. 2A storm drain: rain off the street.
  3. 3One combined sewer carries both, downhill to the shore. Over 90% of the city is served this way.
  4. 4Transport/storage structures ring the shoreline and hold what the plant cannot take yet.
  5. 5Pumped from the storage's floor to a treatment plant.
  6. 6When storage is full it spills over a weir to an outfall at the shore: a combined sewer discharge.

Count it like the lab

The water is diluted one part in ten, sealed into a tray of 97 wells, and kept warm overnight. Each well with Enterococcus in it glows blue under ultraviolet light. Count the glowing big and small wells and a table gives the Most Probable Number per 100 mL. Two samples of the same water rarely read the same: press New sample.

Prototype notes: facts to check before this goes public
  • From the repo, already used on the site: the two plants and their outfall counts (shared/outfalls.py), the four state limits and Surfrider's caution tier (shared/standards.py), when the city began each indicator (Samples and Site Report Card notes), and both charts' numbers (the BeachWatch and CIWQS files).
  • Confirmed by SFPUC's Sewer System Master Plan (Summary Report, Final Draft, March 2010): over 90% of the city is combined; the transport/storage structures ring the perimeter "like a moat", hold flow until the storm passes, and give discharges the equivalent of primary treatment, which is why they are called CSDs; flow is pumped to the plants; the Oceanside plant's outfall runs about four miles offshore (1986) and the Southeast plant's two outfalls go to the bay. Check that the 2010 numbers still hold before quoting any.
  • To confirm: the method in "Count it like the lab", the 97-well tray, one-in-ten dilution and overnight incubation, with both the city lab and Surfrider's.
  • To confirm with a reviewer: each indicator's "what" and "why" lines, and E. coli's "most strains are harmless".
  • Photo sources to ask: SFPUC, the SF Public Library's historical photograph collection, OpenSFHistory. Use only with permission and credit each photo.
