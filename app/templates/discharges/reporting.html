The Discharge Ledger uses three types of report. They do not all measure the same thing. Thus, you cannot always compare a 2013 value with a 2023 value. This page shows all the differences that we know. It then tells how we examine the older records.

What Each Period Contains

San Francisco has two sewer systems. Each system has its own permit and sends its own monthly reports to the Regional Water Board. The Pacific side has 7 outfalls, from Lake Merced to Sea Cliff. The Bay side has 27.

How the Ledger Counts

  • One row is one discharge. In the newer reports, a row is one discharge from one outfall. In the older reports, a row is one day for one outfall on the Pacific side, or one day for one group on the Bay side.
  • A discharge that continues after midnight is one row in the newer reports and two rows in the older reports.
  • Bay volumes start in Oct 2016. Before that date, Bay outfalls show only hours. Yearly volume totals that include the Bay have the mark "incomplete".
  • The live forecast does not use the older records. We trained it on the newer record, which has one row for each event. The Model check grades all models against only that record. Some test models also use the older records. The Model check grades them in the same way. None of them is live.

Different Units and Definitions

  • Hours are decimal until Nov 2012. After that date, they are hours and minutes. The ledger changes the two formats to minutes.
  • Bay outfall groups changed. In 2011, the Islais Creek group included Evans and Hudson. In 2012, these outfalls became separate. In 2013, outfall 031A became part of the Islais group. A group of one outfall shows as that outfall. A larger group shows with a combined name, for example “CSD-018–028 Mission Creek System”.
  • Names changed. Older forms use “CSOD” for an outfall. They use the name “Sea Cliff O.F.” for CSD-006. The ledger uses the current names.
  • Page headers can be incorrect. The header of the Apr 2012 Bay table is “March 2012”. One May 2011 form says “Mary”. The ledger uses the month of the report.

How SFPUC Counted

  • Cover letters count discharges. The tables show days. A discharge that continues for two days is one discharge in the letter and two rows in the table. Thus, {{ q.counts.westside.within_one }} Pacific months have a difference of one.
  • Sometimes SFPUC corrected counts later. The 2016–17 season count decreased from 19 to 18 between the March and April 2017 letters.
  • Revised tables replace the original tables. In Sep 2023, SFPUC sent new Bay tables for Oct 2016 – Oct 2017. The ledger uses the revised tables.
  • Monthly total rows can be incorrect. Sometimes the SFPUC spreadsheet shows the total of an earlier month. Thus, the ledger uses the daily rows.

How We Examine the Data

People typed the older records or read them from scanned PDFs. Thus, we compare each month with an independent source.

How to Examine a Month

  1. Keep the source. Each row gives its report file and page. Thus, anyone can open the original.
  2. Add the values. The total of the outfalls for each day must be equal to the printed day total. The total of the days for each outfall must be equal to its printed column total. The total for the month must be equal to the printed month total.
  3. Compare with the SFPUC counts. The monthly cover letter and the annual report each give the number of discharges. A difference of one usually shows a discharge that continued after midnight.
  4. Compare with rain. A discharge must have rain at the Downtown or Oceanside gauge on the day before, the same day, or the day after. If that day is dry, the row has an incorrect date, or an unusual event occurred. Read the page and the cover letter again.
  5. Compare with independent signals. After 2004, the city sampled Pacheco, Vicente and Fort Funston only after a Pacific discharge. Thus, if no discharge occurred before a sampling trip, the record is possibly incorrect. The State postings and the 2016–17 archive of the SFPUC live feed are two more checks.
  6. Get a second person to read it. A person who did not see the first transcription reads each hand-read page. Then we compare the two transcriptions row by row.
  7. Do not change the source. Keep what SFPUC printed, including “NA” and typing errors. Explain it in the note for the row.
