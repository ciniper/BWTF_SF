The Discharge Ledger joins three kinds of report. They don't all measure the same thing, so a 2013 figure and a 2023 figure aren't always comparable. This page lists every difference we know of, then how the older records are checked.

What each period contains

San Francisco has two sewer systems with their own permits and their own monthly reports to the Regional Water Board. The Pacific side has 7 outfalls, from Lake Merced to Sea Cliff. The Bay side has 27.

How the ledger counts

  • One row is one discharge. In the newer reports that is one outfall's discharge. In the older ones it is one outfall's day on the Pacific side, or one group's day on the Bay side.
  • A discharge that runs past midnight is one row in the newer reports and two in the older ones.
  • Bay volumes start in Oct 2016. Before that, Bay outfalls show hours only, and yearly volume totals that include the Bay are marked incomplete.
  • The live forecast doesn't use the older records. It is trained on the newer per-event record, and the Model check grades every model against that record only. Test models trained on the older records too are graded the same way on the Model check; none is live.

Different units and definitions

  • Hours are decimal through Nov 2012 and hours and minutes after. The ledger converts both to minutes.
  • Bay outfall groups changed. In 2011 the Islais Creek group included Evans and Hudson. In 2012 they were split, and in 2013 outfall 031A joined the Islais group. A group of one outfall appears under that outfall. A larger group appears under a combined name such as “CSD-018–028 Mission Creek System”.
  • Names changed. Older forms say “CSOD” for an outfall and call CSD-006 “Sea Cliff O.F.”. The ledger uses today's names.
  • Page headers can be stale. The Apr 2012 Bay table is headed “March 2012”, and one May 2011 form says “Mary”. The ledger trusts the month the report was filed for.

How SFPUC counted

  • Cover letters count discharges, the tables list days. A discharge over two days is one in the letter and two rows in the table. That is why {{ q.counts.westside.within_one }} Pacific months differ by one.
  • Counts were sometimes corrected later. The 2016–17 season count fell from 19 to 18 between the March and April 2017 letters.
  • Revised tables replace originals. In Sep 2023 SFPUC refiled the Bay tables for Oct 2016 – Oct 2017. The ledger uses the revisions.
  • Monthly total rows can be stale. SFPUC's spreadsheet sometimes carries an earlier month's total, so the ledger trusts the daily rows.

How the data is checked

The older records were typed or read from scanned PDFs, so every month is checked against something produced independently.

How to check a month

  1. Keep the source. Every row names its report file and page, so anyone can open the original.
  2. Add it up. Each day's outfalls must equal the printed day total, each outfall's days its printed column total, and the whole month the printed month total.
  3. Compare with SFPUC's own counts. The monthly cover letter and the annual report each state how many discharges happened. A difference of one usually means a discharge that crossed midnight.
  4. Compare with rain. A discharge needs rain the day before, of or after at the Downtown or Oceanside gauge. A dry day means the row is misdated, or something unusual happened; reread the page and the cover letter.
  5. Compare with independent signals. From 2004 the city sampled Pacheco, Vicente and Fort Funston only after a Pacific discharge, so a sampling trip with no discharge before it is suspect. The State's postings and the 2016–17 archive of SFPUC's live feed are two more checks.
  6. Have a second person read it. Someone who hasn't seen the first transcription reads every hand-read page, and the two are compared row by row.
  7. Don't fix the source. Keep what SFPUC printed, including “NA” and typos, and explain it in the row's note.
