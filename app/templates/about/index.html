What this is

A public dashboard of San Francisco's beach water quality: today's status as the city reports it, a rain-driven forecast of sewage overflows, the lab record from the city and from our volunteers, and the official record of beach postings and discharges.

The Blue Water Task Force is Surfrider's volunteer water-testing program. In San Francisco the chapter samples several beaches every two weeks, with extra rounds during storms, and runs its own lab; this site puts those results next to the city's, and watches the city's beach map so you hear about a posting or a discharge within minutes.

Where the numbers come from

  • SFPUC's live beach map — the posted signs and discharge flags the city shows right now, checked every minute. This drives the Today board and the alerts.
  • SF Gov Open Data — the city lab's bacteria results (Enterococcus, fecal and total coliform) for twenty shoreline stations since July 2020.
  • Surfrider's Blue Water Task Force lab — the volunteers' Enterococcus results and field notes since September 2023.
  • State Water Board BeachWatch — every beach advisory San Francisco filed with the State since 1999.
  • CIWQS self-monitoring reports — every combined-sewer discharge SFPUC reported to regulators since 2016.
  • NOAA rain gauges, NWS forecasts and NOAA tides — the inputs to the overflow forecast and the conditions chips.

How these feeds move from the city's map to your inbox, minute by minute, is drawn out on How it's built. The collection steps behind the records pages are on How we get the records.

What the colours mean

  • Safe — the city's map shows the station meeting California's standards.
  • Caution — an Enterococcus result at or above 36 MPN/100 mL but under the 104 single-sample limit.
  • Posted — SFPUC's sign is up. Usually samples above State standards, sometimes a precaution; repeat samples are collected until it clears.
  • Discharge — SFPUC's combined-sewer-discharge flag; the beach is posted proactively and sampled until it clears.
  • Not sampled — no current result for the station.

Not an official advisory

This is a community-science tool; posted signs and notices from SFPUC or the health department take precedence. SFPUC's own advice: during and immediately after rainfall, nearshore bacteria concentrations may be elevated, even when there has not been a combined sewer discharge.

Beach Hotline: 1-877-SFBEACH (1-877-732-3224) or 415-242-2214. The city's own map is at webapps.sfpuc.org.
