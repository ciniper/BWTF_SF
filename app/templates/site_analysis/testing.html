The Site Report Card grades all sites against the same state limits from 2000 to today. But the tests for those grades changed. The bacteria that the lab measured, the lab method, the frequency of visits to each beach, and the beaches in the program all changed. Remember these changes when you compare one period with a different period.

The Effect on the Grades

  • Early grades use only total coliform. Before {{ mon(d.first_enterococcus.bay) }} at the bay beaches and {{ mon(d.first_enterococcus.ocean) }} at the ocean beaches, the only test was total coliform. This test does not fail frequently. Thus, low failure rates in those years do not mean that the water was cleaner.
  • This site uses a stricter E. coli limit than the SFPUC reports. The report card uses {{ d.ecoli.report_card_limit }} per 100 mL. This is the EPA 1986 limit for freshwater beaches. The California beach rules do not set an E. coli limit. The SFPUC monthly reports use {{ d.ecoli.sfpuc_limit }} for E. coli. This is the state fecal coliform limit. {{ "{:,}".format(d.ecoli.between) }} of {{ "{:,}".format(d.ecoli.results) }} E. coli results are between these two limits. They are failures on this site, but not in the SFPUC reports. This affects 2002 – Mar 2021 and the “any indicator” grade.
  • The stricter total coliform limit applies only from {{ mon(d.datasf_indicators["Fecal coliform"].first) }}. Total coliform fails at 1,000, not 10,000, when fecal coliform is more than one tenth of total coliform. This rule needs a fecal coliform result from the same sample. These results are available only after fecal coliform tests started.
  • More visits after storms change the rates. The city samples routine stations approximately two times more frequently in the days after a discharge. Thus, their failure rates include more bad days. The fixed schedule of three visits each week in 2000–2002 does not have this problem.
  • Stations that the city samples only after discharges do not have a rank. The city samples Pacheco, Vicente and Fort Funston only when the water is probably bad. The report card marks a station "sporadic" when its typical year has fewer than {{ routine_per_year }} samples. It does not give a rank to these stations.
  • New stations have shorter records. The grades for Mission Creek, Islais Creek and Crane Cove Park include fewer years. Thus, a long date range compares them on less data.
