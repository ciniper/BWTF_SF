The Site Report Card grades every site against the same state limits from 2000 to today. The testing behind those grades did not stay the same. The bacteria measured, the lab method, how often each beach was visited and which beaches were sampled all changed. Keep these changes in mind when comparing one period with another.

What it means for the grades

  • Early grades rest on total coliform alone. Before {{ mon(d.first_enterococcus.bay) }} at the bay beaches and {{ mon(d.first_enterococcus.ocean) }} on the ocean beaches, the only test was total coliform, which rarely fails. Low failure rates in those years don't mean cleaner water.
  • E. coli is judged more strictly here than in SFPUC's reports. The report card uses {{ d.ecoli.report_card_limit }} per 100 mL. SFPUC's own monthly reports used {{ d.ecoli.sfpuc_limit }}. {{ "{:,}".format(d.ecoli.between) }} of {{ "{:,}".format(d.ecoli.results) }} E. coli results fall in between: they count as failures here, but not in SFPUC's reports. This affects 2002 – Mar 2021 and the “any indicator” grade.
  • The stricter total coliform limit only applies from {{ mon(d.datasf_indicators["Fecal coliform"].first) }}. Total coliform fails at 1,000 instead of 10,000 when fecal coliform is over a tenth of it. That needs a fecal result from the same sample, which exists only since fecal coliform testing began.
  • Extra visits after storms tilt the rates. Routine stations are sampled about twice as often in the days after a discharge, so their failure rates lean toward bad days. The fixed three-a-week schedule of 2000–2002 doesn't have that tilt.
  • Discharge-only stations aren't ranked. Pacheco, Vicente and Fort Funston are sampled only when the water is likely bad. The report card marks a station sporadic when its typical year has fewer than {{ routine_per_year }} samples, and doesn't rank it.
  • New stations have shorter records. Mission Creek, Islais Creek and Crane Cove Park grades cover fewer years, so a long range compares them on less data.
