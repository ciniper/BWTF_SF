Reading these numbers

"Bad" = {{ graded_on_name }} at or over the state single-sample limit, {{ ssm }} MPN/100mL (the standards; the Standard toggle switches it); the wet season is November–April. City results run from {{ floor }}: SFPUC's lab export ({{ history.label }}) until DataSF begins on {{ datasf_from }}, DataSF from then on. Enterococcus and E. coli were first measured on {{ history.entero_from }}; before August 2020 the city ran E. coli where it now runs fecal coliform, so the fecal-coliform and ratio views have no data for the earlier years.

Weekly regime keeps only the first sample of each site-week (the city's scheduled Monday cadence) — the true week-by-week base rate, and the apples-to-apples comparison with BWTF's own weekly sampling. All samples adds the city's follow-up resamples on later days, which cluster around contamination events and can roughly double a site's rate (Islais Creek: ~16% weekly vs ~30% all-samples over the full record) — it answers "how often did tests fail", not "how often were weeks bad". In both views a site's samples on one calendar day count once, at the day's worse value: the city's dataset carries the date but not the time, so a routine sample and a storm-day resample on the same date cannot be told apart, and the day is graded on its worst bottle. Sites marked sporadic are sampled only occasionally or reactively (often during storm events), so their rates aren't comparable to the weekly-sampled sites and they're listed separately at the bottom, unranked. Also worth knowing: the city samples no station at India Basin — the largest unsampled gap on the east shoreline, between Islais Creek and Sunnydale Cove.

The bacteria standards

California's recreational-water limits (AB 411 / Ocean Plan), in MPN per 100 mL. This page grades on {{ graded_on_name }} by default — the indicator BWTF measures, and the one the newer federal criteria use for marine water — with ≥{{ caution }} as a caution tier below the limit. The Standard toggle regrades on fecal coliform, total coliform, or All (any over): the state posting rule, under which a sample is over standard when any of the three is at or over its limit.
