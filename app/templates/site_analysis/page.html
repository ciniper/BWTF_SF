How to Read These Numbers

"Bad" = {{ graded_on_name }} at or more than the state single-sample limit, {{ ssm }} MPN/100mL (the standards. The Standard toggle changes it.) The wet season is November–April. City results start at {{ floor }}. Until DataSF starts on {{ datasf_from }}, the results come from the SFPUC lab database (STARDB export, Jan 2000 – Jul 2020). After that date, they come from DataSF. The city started to measure Enterococcus and E. coli on {{ history.entero_from }} at the bay stations and on {{ history.entero_from_ocean }} at the ocean beaches. Before these dates, it measured only total coliform. Before April 2021, the city measured E. coli. Now it measures fecal coliform. Thus, the fecal-coliform and ratio views have no data for the earlier years. How testing changed shows all the changes in what the city measured, how it measured, and how frequently.

Weekly Regime uses only the first sample of each site-week (the scheduled Monday frequency of the city). This gives the true weekly rate. It is also more similar to the routine sampling of Surfrider (each two weeks, with more samples during storms). All Samples adds the follow-up resamples that the city takes on later days. These resamples occur after contamination events. They can make the rate of a site approximately two times higher (Islais Creek: approximately 16% weekly, approximately 30% all samples, for the full record). This view tells you how frequently tests failed. It does not tell you how frequently weeks were bad. In the two views, the samples from a site on one calendar day count one time, at the worst value for that day. The city dataset has the date but not the time. Thus, we cannot tell the difference between a routine sample and a storm-day resample on the same date. We grade the day on its worst sample. Sites with the mark Sporadic get samples only sometimes, or only after an event (frequently during storms). Thus, you cannot compare their rates with the sites that get samples each week. The list shows them separately at the bottom, with no rank. Also: the city does not sample a station at India Basin. This is the largest area with no samples on the east shoreline, between Islais Creek and Sunnydale Cove.

The Bacteria Standards

These are the California limits for recreational water (AB 411 / Ocean Plan), in MPN per 100 mL. By default, this page grades on {{ graded_on_name }}. BWTF measures this indicator. The newer federal criteria also use it for marine water. A result of ≥{{ caution }} is in the caution range, below the limit. Use the Standard toggle to grade on fecal coliform, total coliform, or All (Any Over). All (any over) is the state posting rule. With this rule, a sample is more than the standard when one or more of the three indicators is at or more than its limit.
