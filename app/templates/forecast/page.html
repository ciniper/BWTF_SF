{% call page_head("CSO Forecast", "Sewage-overflow risk for San Francisco's beaches, by zone, updated every 30 minutes.", "today", "How this forecast works") %}

What it is. For each beach zone, the chance that a sewage overflow has fouled the water: today and the next five days, as a percentage, refreshed every 30 minutes. It is a rain model. It does not see dry-weather dirtiness, and posted signs always win.

Stage 1: how much rain? What fell comes from two NOAA gauges, Downtown and Oceanside; the last few hours from the SFO airport gauge; the hours ahead from a weather model (ICON, via Open-Meteo). On past days the gauges replace everything else.

Stage 2: will the sewers overflow? San Francisco has four combined-sewer basins. For each one, a model trained on ten years of the city's filed overflow reports turns the rain pattern (the day's total, the last days' totals, the peak hour) into the chance of an overflow that day.

Stage 3: which beaches? An overflow reaches the beaches its outfalls post, and not every outfall spills when its basin does: each beach gets the basin's chance times the share of the basin's overflows of that size on which its own outfalls spilled, from the city's records.

Stage 4: for how long? An overflow fouls a beach for days. How long, by basin and by the size of the overflow, comes from the city's own bacteria samples after past overflows. The percentage shown is the combined risk from the last eight days of overflow chances.

Stage 5: live corrections. When SFPUC's beach map shows an overflow flag, that day becomes certain and the following days follow; when it shows a posting or a clean sample, the following days are held up or capped. Nothing observed, nothing changed. The toggle on the live view shows the model alone.

Reading the number. The word with each percentage is a fixed risk level, the same on every page: {% for l in RISK_LEVELS.levels %}{{ l.label }} {{ l.lo }}–{{ l.hi }}%{{ ', ' if not loop.last else '.' }}{% endfor %} No alert reads this number: alerts follow what the city's beach map shows.

Is it any good? The Model check tab builds a forecast from its five stages: pick a part for each and see how good it is on its own, and what the parts make together. Day by day, it grades every past day against the city's filed overflows and its samples, zone by zone, at each risk level. Two plain reports go deeper: the forecast, stage by stage (how good each part is) and how the forecast works (what each part does). Every report, current and archived, is listed in one place.
