Stage 2: from discharge probability to beach risk

Stage 1 says how likely each plant is to discharge today. Stage 2 turns that, and the past week of it, into the chance a beach group is fouled today — using how long beaches actually stayed dirty after real discharges. This page opens that layer up for the served v4 set and every candidate set: what every model shares, what differs, and a walk-through of any day in the hindcast.

The transformation

For a beach group on day D, take today's and each of the previous seven days' discharge probability for the group's basin. Multiply each by the impact fraction x(k, size): the share of sample days that were over standard k days after a discharge of that size, with the dry-weather background removed. Each product is "the chance that day's discharge is fouling the beach today". Assume independence, multiply the chances that each one is not, and take one minus the product. A discharge day itself is a stay-out day by rule, so x(0) = 1 and the group risk on a discharge day is never below the basin probability.

riskgroup(D) = 1 − ∏k=0..7 ( 1 − pbasin(D−k) · x(k, size(D−k)) ) x(0, ·) = 1 x(k, size) = (ptable(k, size) − baseline) / (1 − baseline) attributable P(elevated), background removed size = predicted discharge volume V (MG) from the basin's volume head → blend weight w = V / (V + median event) x(k, V) = w · xlarge(k) + (1 − w) · xsmall(k) riskzone(D) = max over the zone's groups

What is shared, and what differs per model

Every model set — the served v4 and each candidate — runs through the same stage 2: the same impact table, the same volume heads, the same composition code. What differs is what stage 1 feeds in: the daily probabilities, and the rain source each basin model reads. The volume heads read the rain source they were trained on regardless of which stage-1 set is in play.

The impact table

Fitted once, at v4 training, from every sample day within a week of a CIWQS discharge: for each beach group, size class and days-since bucket, the share of sample days over standard, with the count behind it. Serving smooths each small/large curve to be non-increasing over days (weighted pool-adjacent-violators), because contamination decays and cells with 3–6 samples are noisy enough to read day 3 above day 1.

Cells: P(elevated) with n underneath; hover for the attributable value. Curves: attributable x(k) — solid large, dashed small, orange dots the blend at the chosen volume.

Outfall split: a stage 2 variant

Stage 1 predicts one probability per basin, but a basin's outfalls post different beaches: on the Westside, three outfalls post Ocean Beach and four post Baker and China Beach; in North Shore, four post Crissy Field and three post Aquatic Park. The v4 composition gives both groups the basin's whole probability, so a Sea Cliff discharge turns Ocean Beach red. The outfall_split_v1 variant multiplies the basin probability by the share of that basin's CIWQS discharge days, by size class, on which at least one of the group's own outfalls discharged, and refits the impact table on those group-attributed days. Where a group's outfalls are the whole basin (Mission Creek, Southeast) the share is 1 and nothing changes.

Volume heads: the "size" input

A gradient-boosted regressor per basin predicts log(1 + MG) from the same 19 rain features, trained only on events with a reported volume. Its only job in stage 2 is the blend weight w = V / (V + median event), which saturates fast: above a few times the median, every discharge is "large".
