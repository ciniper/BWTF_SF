What was compared

Four sets, two stage 1s × two stage 2s. Stage 1 gb_v1 is the gradient-boosted trees the served bundle runs; logit_v1 is the weights model (19 rain features plus hinge terms, L2 logistic). Stage 2 v1 composes the basin probability directly; v2 first multiplies it by the share of the basin's discharge days on which the beach group's own outfalls took part (the outfall split), which only bites on the Westside and North Shore. All four are scored on the same days, the same labels, the same volume heads, and the same rain treatment (post-training days with the gauge-outage rule). The served set is gb_v1 + stage 2 v1.

The verdict, and why

Under your setting — a miss costs two false alarms, 50% line, every out-of-sample day — logit_v1_s2v2 is king: cost 121 against 132 for logit_v1 and gb_v1_s2v2 (tied) and 140 for the served gb_v1, a 14% cut. The arithmetic behind the crown: it raises 31 fewer false alarms than the served set (91 against 122), which saves 31 points, and misses 6 more discharge days (63 against 69 of 78), which costs 12. On clean-day false alarms, the ones that are the model's own fault rather than the tail after a real discharge, it is 18 against 40. Every one of the six extra misses is a small Westside event: the volume it catches is 95.6% of all discharged sewage against 97.8% for the served set.

The two layers contribute about equally and almost additively. Holding stage 1 fixed, stage 2 v2 removes 15–16 false alarms for 2–4 catches, a net 8–11 points under this weighting. Holding stage 2 fixed, the weights stage 1 does the same again. Stack both and you get the king. But note what the flipped weighting did to the margin: under the old (false alarm = 2 misses) setting the king cut the served set's cost by 22%; under this one by 14%, and it is the set that misses the most. The rest of this page is about how far that crown can be trusted.

Where each set wins and loses (50% line, out of sample)

North Shore is a clean win for the weights stage 1: every set catches all 11 discharge days, but gb_v1 raises 6 false alarms and logit_v1 raises 2. The split does nothing here at 50% (it helps at 25%: 40 → 29 false alarms for gb_v1).

Ocean Beach is where the split earns its keep: 22 false alarms (gb_v1) → 13 (gb_v1_s2v2) → 10 (logit_v1_s2v2), for one to two lost catches out of 16. Zone cost 26 → 21 → 16; the king's clean-day false alarms there are 1 against the served set's 11.

Baker and China is the trade-off zone and the king does not win it: gb_v1 catches 17 of 23 for 14 false alarms (cost 26), gb_v1_s2v2 15 of 23 for 7 (cost 23, the cheapest), logit_v1_s2v2 13 of 23 for 5 (cost 25). The lost catches are the sub-MG Sea Cliff #2 trickles on under half an inch of rain, which citywide rain cannot see; under a weighting that charges two per miss, the trees' habit of over-calling small storms is accidentally useful here.

The East is the cost centre no set touches, and under this weighting it is 59–64% of every set's total: 80 false alarms for the trees, 74 for the weights model (65 and 62 of them in the tail after a real discharge), for 27 and 26 of 28 caught; zone cost 82 and 78. The split is the identity there by construction. Most of that cost is the composition holding risk up for a week while Islais Creek is in fact still dirty, plus the East's 35% dry-weather baseline. That is a stage 2 and non-rain problem, not a stage 1 ranking problem, and it caps what any of these four sets can do.

Stage 1 on its own

logit_v1 ranks storms better than gb_v1 in every basin on the holdout (Westside 0.854 vs 0.828, North Shore 0.963 vs 0.953, Central 0.977 vs 0.967, Southeast 0.923 vs 0.836 PR-AUC) and out of sample overall, and its percentages are more honest: out of sample, days it put at 25–50% discharged 44% of the time and 50–75% days 70%, where gb_v1's 25–50% band discharged only 18% of the time and its 50–75% band 53%. For gb_v1 the 50% line is doing the work a 25% line should; for logit_v1 the bands mean what they say. That calibration gap is exactly why the trees catch more trickles at 50%: they say 60% when they should say 30%. The one place gb_v1 ranks better is Southeast since training (0.802 vs 0.742): logit_v1's C and rain source were picked on the holdout, and some of its Southeast lead there was selection optimism. It flips nothing in the East's cost, which is the tail rather than the ranking, but it is the reason not to swap Southeast's stage 1 blindly.

How robust is the verdict?

Across weightings, at the 50% line: logit_v1_s2v2 has the lowest cost from "1 : 1" through "a miss costs 5 false alarms" (166 against 167 for the served set at 5 — a tie in practice). From a miss costing 6 false alarms the served gb_v1 is cheapest at 50% (176 against 181), and by 10 it is clearly ahead (212 against 241). At the 25% line logit_v1_s2v2 is cheapest under every weighting tried, up to a miss costing 15 false alarms, because at 25% the false alarms are so many that any cut in them dominates. Counting clean-day false alarms only, the king holds at 50% (48 against 53, 54 and 58).

The forward test does not confirm the crown at 50%. On the since-training window alone (Nov 2025 → Aug 2026, 29 discharge days) the order at 50% is logit_v1 = gb_v1_s2v2 at 49, the served gb_v1 at 51, and logit_v1_s2v2 last at 52: it misses 10 of 29 discharge days (8 of 13 on Baker–China, 2 of 6 on Ocean Beach) against the served set's 6, and catches 89.5% of that window's volume against 94%. One of its two Ocean Beach misses is the Feb 19 2026 event (48 MG), which stage 2 v1 catches through the tail of Feb 16–17 and the split lets fall under the line. At the 25% line the same window favours it clearly (72 against 91). Three points on 29 events is one storm, but the direction is the caution: the king's advantage comes from the holdout, where the weights model's hyper-parameters were chosen.

The line matters more than the set

Every set's cheapest single line is still 75% under this weighting (gb_v1 98, logit_v1 101, gb_v1_s2v2 111, logit_v1_s2v2 112), because quiet days outnumber discharge days about fifty to one and two-for-one does not overturn that. And at 75% the order inverts: the served trees are cheapest, since once only the confident days are alarmed the sets' false alarms converge and the trees' extra catches decide it. At 60% logit_v1 is cheapest (104). Per zone, the king's own cheapest lines are 50% on Ocean Beach and Baker–China, 60% on North Shore and 75% on the East; the served set's are 75 / 60 / 75 / 75. Sum each set's per-zone minima and they land within four points of each other: logit_v1 89, gb_v1_s2v2 90, gb_v1 91, logit_v1_s2v2 93 — against a spread of 19 between the sets at a flat 50%. Choosing the line per zone is worth 30–50 points; choosing the set at a fixed 50% is worth 19. (Per-zone minima picked on the evaluation data flatter every set equally; read them as a ceiling.) Steeper weightings pull the line down: at a miss costing 4 false alarms the cheapest single line is 60%; at 10 it is 40–60% and the East's own minimum drops to 50–60%.

Caveats

Seventy-eight discharge days is a small denominator; differences of two or three catches are one storm. The post-training window is a single wet season. logit_v1's hyper-parameters were chosen on the holdout, so its holdout numbers flatter it, and the "since training" table is the fairer one — it favours the weights model on cost only at 25%. Every catch here counts a 0.07 MG trickle like a 48 MG discharge; the volume view narrows the gap between sets to two percentage points. And none of this grades the live overrides (observed CSO flags) — that needs the live-mode replay evaluation in TODO.
