What was compared, and against what

Four sets, two stage 1s × two stage 2s. Stage 1 gb_v1 is the gradient-boosted trees the served bundle runs; logit_v1 is the weights model (19 rain features plus hinge terms, L2 logistic). Stage 2 v1 composes the basin probability directly; v2 first multiplies it by the share of the basin's discharge days on which the beach group's own outfalls took part (the outfall split). All four are scored on the same days, the same volume heads and the same rain treatment. The served set is gb_v1 + stage 2 v1.

Three rulers, in the order the page shows them. Discharge days + samples is primary (Chase, 2026-09-26): stage 1 is graded on discharge days alone; the final percentage on what it claims — a beach fouled by a discharge — so the discharge day is bad, an elevated sample in the week after one is bad (persistence, confirmed by the water), a clean sample is good, an unsampled day in that week is not graded, and an elevated sample with no recent discharge is dry-weather dirtiness the rain model does not claim, counted but not scored. Discharge days only is the ruler the first version of this report used; it charges the model for every day it stays up after a discharge. Beach postings (the State's BeachWatch record) is kept as a third view. Out of sample the primary ruler grades 182 bad days — 78 discharge days and 104 confirmed-persistence days — leaves 239 unsampled tail days ungraded, and sets aside 156 dry-weather elevated samples, of which the models flag 9 to 16.

The verdict, and why it depends on the line

At your 50% line, the trees with the split are cheapest: gb_v1_s2v2 at 139, the served gb_v1 at 144, then logit_v1_s2v2 at 155 and logit_v1 at 158. The weights sets lose here because they miss more confirmed-persistence days in the East (53 to 55 of 104 against 60) and more discharge days (63 to 65 of 78 against 65 to 69), and two-for-one misses outweigh their 10 to 15 fewer false alarms.

At each set's own cheapest line, logit_v1_s2v2 is king by a wide margin: 116 at 25%, against 126 for logit_v1 at 30%, 139 for gb_v1_s2v2 at 50% and 140 for gb_v1 at 30%. Nothing the trees can do at any line gets within 20 points of it. At 25% it catches 156 of 182 bad days (70 of 78 discharge days, 86 of 104 confirmed-persistence days) for 64 false alarms, 44 of them on clean-sample days and 20 on quiet days.

Both statements are true, and the calibration section explains why they differ. The trees say 60% on days the weights model says 30%; out of sample the trees' 25 to 50% band discharged 18% of the time and the weights model's 44%. A "50% line" is therefore a different threshold for each family, and comparing sets at a fixed line rewards the one that inflates. The fair comparison is each set at its own best line, and there the weights model with the split wins under every miss weight from one to ten (at six it ties logit_v1 within three points).

The forward test is the caution, as before. Since training at 50% the trees are cheapest (gb_v1 and gb_v1_s2v2 at 38, logit_v1 40, logit_v1_s2v2 43); at 25% logit_v1_s2v2 is cheapest at 45. On 50 bad days a seven-point gap is a handful of storms, and the holdout, where the weights model's settings were chosen, is where its lead is largest (71 against 101 at each set's best line).

Where each set wins and loses (primary ruler, each zone at its own cheapest line)

The East is where the confirmed-persistence days live (80 of the 104, because SFPUC samples posted beaches daily) and it wants a low line for every set: 25 to 30%. There the weights model catches 96 of 102 bad days for 27 false alarms (cost 39) and the trees 91 of 102 for 27 (cost 49). Under this ruler the East is no longer a cost centre: its tail alarms were right.

Ocean Beach is the split's zone: logit_v1_s2v2 at 50% catches 19 of 30 for 2 false alarms (cost 24); gb_v1 at 30% catches 26 of 30 for 23 (cost 31). Baker and China costs 36 to 39 whatever runs; the misses are sub-MG Sea Cliff #2 trickles. North Shore favours the weights model with the split at 25% (17 of 18 for 12 alarms, cost 14) over the trees at 75% (10 of 18 for 1, cost 17).

Stage 1 on its own

Stage 1 is graded on discharge days, where that label is exactly right. logit_v1 ranks storms better than gb_v1 in every basin on the holdout (Westside 0.854 vs 0.828, North Shore 0.963 vs 0.953, Central 0.977 vs 0.967, Southeast 0.923 vs 0.836 PR-AUC) and out of sample overall, and its percentages mean what they say: days it put at 25–50% discharged 44% of the time and 50–75% days 70%, where gb_v1's 25–50% band discharged 18% of the time and its 50–75% band 53%. The one place gb_v1 ranks better is Southeast since training (0.802 vs 0.742): logit_v1's C and rain source were picked on the holdout, and some of its Southeast lead there was selection optimism. That is the reason not to swap Southeast's stage 1 blindly.

How robust is the verdict?

At each set's own cheapest line, logit_v1_s2v2 has the lowest cost under "1 : 1", "a miss costs 2, 3, 4 and 10 false alarms", and is within three points of logit_v1 at 6. At a fixed 50% line the trees are cheapest under every weighting, gb_v1_s2v2 up to a miss costing 3 and the served gb_v1 from 4 up — the calibration gap, not a different model. Counting the same alarms against discharge days only reverses the picture (that ruler's cheapest line is 75% and its king at 50% is logit_v1_s2v2); against postings the cheapest line drops to 15–25% and the plain weights model wins at 25%. The direction all three agree on is that 50% is too high for the weights family and about right for the trees.

The line matters as much as the set

Under the primary ruler the cheapest single line is 25% for logit_v1_s2v2, 30% for logit_v1 and gb_v1, 50% for gb_v1_s2v2; per zone the minima sit at 25 to 30% in the East for every set, 25 to 50% on Ocean Beach, 30 to 60% on Baker–China and 25 to 75% on North Shore. Summing each set's per-zone minima: logit_v1_s2v2 114, logit_v1 116, gb_v1_s2v2 132, gb_v1 134 — the set is worth about 20 points and the line about 30, the reverse of what the discharge-day ruler said. Per-zone minima chosen on the evaluation data flatter every set equally; read them as a ceiling.

Graded against the signs

The State's BeachWatch record (through Feb 2026; 267 posted days for a sewage or rain cause out of sample) is the third ruler. It agrees with the primary one on the two things that matter and disagrees on one. It agrees that the "false alarms" after a discharge were mostly right — at 50% the served set's 122 become 43 alarms on days no beach was posted, the East's 80 become 9 — and that the line should come down: its cheapest line is 15 to 25% for every set. It disagrees on the split: at 25% gb_v1_s2v2 catches 33 of 50 Ocean Beach posted days to gb_v1's 40 for 9 fewer alarms, a small net loss, where the primary ruler scores the split a clear win. The difference is what counts as bad on Ocean Beach after a Sea Cliff-only discharge: SFPUC posts it, the samples mostly come back clean. Postings are precautionary — on sampled CSO-posted days the water was over standard 47 to 83% of the time — so the primary ruler stays with the water.

Caveats

Seventy-eight discharge days and 104 confirmed-persistence days is a small denominator; differences of a few catches are one storm. The confirmed-persistence days are where SFPUC resampled, which is mostly the East. The post-training window is a single wet season. logit_v1's hyper-parameters were chosen on the holdout, so its holdout numbers flatter it, and the "since training" table is the fairer one — it favours the trees at 50% and the weights model at 25%. Every catch counts a 0.07 MG trickle like a 48 MG discharge; the volume view corrects for that. Dry-weather dirtiness — 156 elevated samples out of sample with no recent discharge, mostly in the East — is real and outside every set here; that is the non-rain work. And none of this grades the live overrides (observed CSO flags, samples as they arrive) — that needs the live-mode replay.
