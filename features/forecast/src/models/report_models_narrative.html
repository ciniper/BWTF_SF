What was compared

Four sets, two stage 1s × two stage 2s. Stage 1 gb_v1 is the gradient-boosted trees the served v4 runs; logit_v1 is the weights model (19 rain features plus hinge terms, L2 logistic). Stage 2 v1 composes the basin probability directly; v2 first multiplies it by the share of the basin's discharge days on which the beach group's own outfalls took part (the outfall split), which only bites on the Westside and North Shore. All four are scored on the same days, the same labels, the same volume heads, and since 2026-09-25 the same rain treatment (post-training days with the gauge-outage rule).

The verdict, and why

Under your setting — a false alarm costs two misses, 50% line, every out-of-sample day — logit_v1_s2v2 is king: cost 197 against 253 for the served v4, a 22% cut. It gets there almost entirely on false alarms: 91 against 122, and on clean-day false alarms, the ones that are the model's own fault rather than the tail after a real discharge, 18 against 40. It pays with 6 fewer discharge days caught out of 78 (63 vs 69), and every one of those six is a small Westside event: the volume it catches is 95.6% of all discharged sewage against v4's 97.8%.

The two layers contribute about equally and almost additively. Holding stage 1 fixed, stage 2 v2 removes 15–16 false alarms at the cost of 2–4 catches. Holding stage 2 fixed, the weights stage 1 does the same again: 15–16 fewer false alarms for 2–4 catches. Stack both and you get the king.

Where each set wins and loses

North Shore is a clean win for the weights stage 1: every set catches all 11 discharge days, but gb_v1 raises 6 false alarms at 50% and logit_v1 raises 2. The split does nothing here at 50% (it helps at 25%: 40 → 29 false alarms for v4).

Ocean Beach is where the split earns its keep: 22 false alarms (v4) → 13 (gb + split) → 10 (logit + split), for one to two lost catches out of 16. Baker and China is the trade-off zone: the split and the weights model both lose catches there (17 → 13 of 23 across the four sets), and those are the sub-MG Sea Cliff #2 trickles that citywide rain cannot see. The false alarms fall 14 → 5 in exchange.

The East is the cost centre no set touches: 74–80 false alarms at 50%, the single largest term in every total, and the split is the identity there by construction. Most of them are the tail after a real discharge — the composition holding risk up for a week while Islais Creek is in fact still dirty — plus the East's 35% dry-weather baseline. That is a stage 2 and non-rain problem, not a stage 1 ranking problem, and it caps what any of these four sets can do.

Stage 1 on its own

logit_v1 ranks storms better than gb_v1 in every basin on the holdout (Westside 0.854 vs 0.828, North Shore 0.963 vs 0.953, Central 0.977 vs 0.967, Southeast 0.923 vs 0.836 PR-AUC) and out of sample overall, and its percentages are more honest: out of sample, days it put at 25–50% discharged 44% of the time and 50–75% days 70%, where gb_v1's 25–50% band discharged only 18% of the time. For gb_v1 the 50% line is doing the work a 25% line should; for logit_v1 the bands mean what they say. The one place gb_v1 is better is Southeast since training (0.802 vs 0.742): logit_v1's C and rain source were picked on the holdout, and some of its Southeast lead there was selection optimism. That flips nothing in the zone costs because the East's cost is the tail, not the ranking, but it is the reason not to swap Southeast's stage 1 blindly.

How robust is the verdict?

logit_v1_s2v2 has the lowest cost at both the 50% and the 25% line under every weighting from "a false alarm costs 2 misses" through "a miss costs 4 false alarms", and whether or not tail false alarms are counted. The order only flips once a miss costs roughly six or more false alarms, at which point v4 wins on raw catches. On the "since training" window alone, logit_v1 without the split ties logit_v1_s2v2 on cost (74 vs 74) with two more catches, because at 50% the split's small-event tails are mostly already under the line; the split's value since training shows at 25% (77 → 60 false alarms for one catch). Since training the split sets also miss the Feb 19 2026 event (48 MG) at 50%, which stage 2 v1 catches through the tail of Feb 16–17: the split sets catch 89.5–90% of that window's volume against 94% for the v1 sets. That is the honest cost of the split at the 50% line.

Caveats

Seventy-eight discharge days is a small denominator; differences of two or three catches are one storm. The post-training window is a single wet season. logit_v1's hyper-parameters were chosen on the holdout, so its holdout numbers flatter it a little; the "since training" table is the fairer one and it still favours the weights model on cost. Every catch here counts a 0.07 MG trickle like a 48 MG discharge; the volume view corrects for that and narrows the gap between sets to two percentage points. And none of this grades the live overrides (observed CSO flags) — that needs the live-mode replay evaluation in TODO.
