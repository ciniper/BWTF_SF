Rain sources

Two NOAA daily gauges define "rain" for training and for every past day; live hours come from the SFO airport gauge and the ECMWF forecast. Each basin model was trained on one rain source and is served the same one.

The two gauges (NOAA COOP via ACIS)
Which rain each basin model sees

Chosen per basin at training time: the local gauge was adopted only where it beat the two-gauge mean on the chronological holdout by ≥ 0.01 PR-AUC without a worse Brier score. Fill rule when a gauge day is missing: the other gauge, then the hourly model total, then 0.

Daily totals in inches. Shaded spans: one gauge reported exactly 0.00 for ≥ 2 consecutive days while the other gauge totalled ≥ 0.5" — a dead gauge fed as zeros, not a dry spell. Westside and Southeast run on the two-gauge mean, so they see half the rain during an Oceanside outage.

Volume heads: how big would the discharge be?

A second gradient-boosted model per basin (a regressor on log(1+MG)) estimates the expected discharge volume from the same 19 features. Stage 2 uses it to pick between the "small" and "large" persistence curves. Trained only on events with a reported volume.

Stage 2: from discharge probability to beach risk

A discharge contaminates beaches for days. The impact table is empirical: for each beach group, how often its samples were over standard k days after a discharge, split by discharge size, with the dry-weather background removed. The displayed risk composes the last 8 days of discharge probabilities with that decay. A discharge day itself is always a stay-out day (x(0) = 1).

risk(D) = 1 − ∏k=0..7 ( 1 − pdischarge(D−k) · x(k, size(D−k)) ) with x(0, ·) = 1 x(k, size) = attributable P(elevated) = (ptable − baseline) / (1 − baseline), blended small↔large by volume / (volume + median event)
Smoothing = weighted pool-adjacent-violators: each curve is forced non-increasing over days since discharge, because contamination decays.

Basins → groups → zones

Outfalls belong to a drainage basin (the model unit). Beaches are grouped by which outfalls post them (the impact-table unit). A zone, what subscribers pick and the forecast page leads with, is the worst of its groups.
